# TDB Brand Bible

The distilled voice reference. Consult this first for any ask of the form "write copy that represents TDB to an audience" — social posts, group intros, bios, pitches, boilerplate, new-surface copy. It condenses guidance scattered across a dozen docs; the **source map at the bottom** says where each rule lives in full. When a voice rule changes, update the source doc *and* this distillation.

**The one-sentence version:** TDB is a warm, friendly, one-person local news site covering the Tucson-metro news that falls through the cracks — helpful and positive by default, rigorous underneath, never a scold.

## Canonical strings (use verbatim — do not paraphrase these)

- **Wordmark:** Tucson Daily Brief · **Initialism:** TDB
- **Masthead tagline:** "The Tucson news you'd otherwise miss, by Nicholas De Leon."
- **Masthead kicker:** "From the Old Pueblo"
- **Footer signature:** "By Nicholas De Leon, in Tucson."
- **Mission sentence (About):** "Tucson Daily Brief reads the agenda packets, watches the council meetings, and tracks the public filings — and brings you the Tucson stories hiding in the public record, every morning."
- **The gap thesis:** Tucson is one of the most under-covered metros in the country relative to its size; more public business happens here in any given week than existing outlets can reach. TDB exists to close that gap.
- **Newsletter promise:** "Feel more caught up on Tucson every Sunday." / "A warm Sunday-morning roundup of Tucson, in your inbox." / "Free. Sunday mornings. Unsubscribe anytime."
- **Daily Briefs promise:** "The fastest way to feel caught up before breakfast."
- **The warm formulation of the moat:** "We read the agenda so you don't have to" — never "here's what they buried."

## The three registers

Three voices coexist by design. Never blend them; pick the register for the surface.

| Register | Sounds like | Surfaces | Anchor rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warm / light** | A Tucson neighbor writing to you on a Sunday morning | Newsletter, IG/FB, Only in Tucson shorts, crossword, mascot, About byline, community intros, subscribe copy | Maximally helpful, positive attitude; kitchen-table not civic-tech; make them smile; no caveat-stacking |
| **Neutral / watchful** | A public-records reporter, not an activist | Daily brief, What to Watch, What They Decided, Buried in the Agenda, In Depth | AP style, no editorializing, no hype words, attribute everything, soft-hedge single-source items |
| **Industry / AI story** | The tool-assisted-speedrun essay | About "How this is made," LinkedIn, industry conversations ONLY | Never leads consumer copy. Different audience, keep the wires uncrossed |

**The two-brand split is the master rule:** reader-facing surfaces lead with the *outcome* (the news you'd otherwise miss); the AI/tooling story belongs on About, LinkedIn, and industry conversations. Don't lead consumer marketing with "AI-assisted journalism."

## The schoolmarm rule (light vs heavy)

TDB is a civic monitor, not a schoolmarm. This is a **survival constraint, not a preference** — newsrooms that pitch everything in the heavy register go out of business.

- Lead with being helpful and positive, especially on social. Instagram is a positive platform: "Fiber internet is coming to Tucson" beats a card about what the company isn't saying, even when both are accurate.
- **Rigor is practiced, not displayed.** Verify to the full bar; attribute lightly on the surface; keep the sourcing trail in the caption/body.
- Failure shapes to watch for: turning a neutral item into an exposé, stacking caveats, media criticism, "they don't want you to know" energy. If a piece's job is to inform, let it inform.
- Heavy work still ships — accountability reporting is the moat — it just doesn't set the tone on every surface. The dark/tense treatment stays reserved for Buried in the Agenda.
- Weekend rule: newsletter promo + light/fun only.

## Banned phrases (reader-facing surfaces)

Civic-tech / backstage vocabulary that breaks the voice — the models leak these by default:

> "public records," "agenda mining," "local intelligence," "monitoring the situation," "our review," "our pipeline," "flagged by," "surfaced from," "according to filings," "per the data," "based on the agenda materials"

Also banned everywhere: un-sourced hype words ("shocking," "unbelievable," "nobody expected," "changed everything," "they don't want you to know"). Describe what actually happened; let the facts carry it.

Write as a person who reads the news closely, not a system that processes it.

## Universal constraints (all registers)

- **No fabrication in reader-facing output, ever.** A wrong fact on a public card spreads faster than a buried blog line.
- **The accountable human is the draw.** For a local news account, legitimacy + locality is the moat: "there's a real, accountable person behind this brief." Show the outcome + the human, never the machinery (nobody wants to look at a terminal).
- **Interactivity beat:** every social package gets something the reader can answer **from their own life, not from civic knowledge** ("How fast is your internet?" ✅ / "What do you think of the IDA restructuring?" ❌). The reliable shape is a number the reader can compare themselves against. Don't force it — no hollow "thoughts?"
- **Card vs caption:** the card is a hook (headline + a dek with 2–3 concrete details — not bare, not a summary); the caption carries the story, numbers, sourcing, link. Never say the same thing twice.
- **Attribution lives in the caption/body,** not on the card.
- Emoji: sparing to none. Section emojis in the daily brief are structural; elsewhere at most one tasteful emoji, none in neutral-register beats.

## Introducing TDB to new audiences — boilerplate

Approved self-descriptions, warmest register. Adapt freely but keep: outcome-first, free/no-ads/just-me, plain English, the accountable human, and end with an ask the reader can answer from their own street.

**One-liner:**
> Tucson Daily Brief — a free morning read of the Tucson news that tends to fall through the cracks, plus the big stories everyone's talking about. By Nicholas De Leon.

**Short paragraph:**
> I'm Nicholas, and I make Tucson Daily Brief: a free daily brief (email, web, and podcast) of Tucson-metro news you'd otherwise miss — what the city, county, Marana, and Oro Valley are deciding, what's opening near you, and the day's big Southern Arizona stories. No ads, no paywall, just me. I read the agendas so you don't have to.

**Community/FB-group intro (full):** see the worked example in `MARKETING.md` → distribution notes, or rebuild from the rules above. Mechanics for groups: post from the personal profile, not the Page; read the pinned rules first (many groups quarantine link posts to a weekly thread); vary wording between groups and stagger over days — identical cross-posted text is a spam signal; where "business" is a stretch, say so cheerfully ("not really a business — it's free and it's just me — but I hope it fits the spirit of the group").

## Naming style

Reader-facing names sit in a single plain-spoken register — clarity for a first-time reader over cleverness ("people need to know exactly what they're looking at"): Daily Briefs, What to Watch, What They Decided, Around Town, In Depth. ChatTDB is the one allowed bit of product sparkle, and it's clear. Brand-flavored names (The Tucson Mini, The Old Pueblo Speaks) tie to place and masthead. The site leans lightly Spanish-bilingual (Old Pueblo, mirador candidates) — natural Sonoran register, never costume. Collision blacklist for new names: Sentinel, Spotlight, Star, Weekly, Inside, Local/Explorer.

## Visual voice (the tonal equivalent)

Warm organic / Southwest: "an editorial magazine published from a thick-walled adobe building in Barrio Viejo — warm, slow, place-aware, restrained, never busy." Warm-only palette (no blues/teals/purples), Fraunces + Newsreader, the drifting sun-cast. The site should feel like *a place*, not a personal blog with extras.
