# Marana Town Council Meeting Preview: August 4, 2026

The Marana Town Council convenes Tuesday for a regular meeting that includes certification of last month's primary election results, updates to water and wastewater infrastructure fee plans, and police cooperation agreements with state and federal agencies that touch on immigration enforcement. The council will also take the first steps toward settling two active lawsuits in closed session.

## Top Items to Watch

### 1. Council to Certify July 21 Primary Election Results
**Item A2 | Council Action**

The council will formally declare and adopt the results of Marana's July 21, 2026 primary election — a consequential step that sets the field for any general election races in the fall. Whoever is advancing — or has won outright — in contests for mayor or council seats will be officially determined at this meeting. Given Marana's rapid growth and the politically charged debates over data center zoning and a proposed ICE detention facility, the composition of the next council carries significant stakes for residents across the northwest Tucson metro.

### 2. Marana Police Renew GIITEM Agreement — With Immigration Enforcement in the Name
**Item C5 | Consent Agenda**

The council is set to reauthorize Marana PD's participation in the **Gang and Immigration Intelligence Team Enforcement Mission (GIITEM)**, a statewide Arizona DPS task force. This item lands squarely in the middle of the ongoing regional debate over Marana's relationship with federal immigration enforcement — particularly as the town weighs hosting an ICE detention facility at the former state prison site. Civil liberties advocates and immigrant communities in the Tucson metro have long scrutinized GIITEM participation, making this a quietly significant item buried in the consent agenda.

### 3. Town Faces Two Active Lawsuits — Closed-Door Settlement Talks Scheduled
**Items E2 & E3 | Executive Session**

The council will meet privately to discuss settlement in two separate Pima County Superior Court cases: **Unrein v. Town of Marana** (C20254246) and **Town of Marana v. Barker Contracting, Inc.** (C20236109). The Unrein case name is worth watching closely given the town's recent data center rezoning controversy, in which referendum petitions were rejected and lawsuits followed. The Barker Contracting case suggests a construction or public works dispute. Taxpayers have a financial stake in how — and how much — both cases are resolved.

### 4. Wastewater Impact Fee Plan Amended as Development Pressure Grows
**Item A1 | Council Action**

Staff is asking the council to amend Marana's 2022 Wastewater Facilities Infrastructure Improvements Plan, revising the size, alignment, and projected costs of two gravity pipe projects. Critically, the total wastewater impact fee charged to developers would not change — but how that money is allocated among projects would shift. As Marana absorbs thousands of new homes along the I-10 corridor, decisions about wastewater infrastructure capacity directly determine how fast and where growth can occur, and whether existing residents bear the burden of new connections.

### 5. Final Plat Approved for Marana Air Commerce Park II — Drainage Project Added to Capital Plan
**Item C2 | Consent Agenda**

The council is set to approve the final plat for **Marana Air Commerce Park II, Lot 2**, near Avra Valley Road east of Sandario Road — and simultaneously add a new **drainage channel project** to the town's five-year Capital Improvement Plan for FY 2027–2028. Industrial and commercial development near the Marana Regional Airport has been a key part of the town's economic diversification strategy. Adding a drainage project to the CIP in the same motion signals that stormwater infrastructure is already a concern for this site, worth tracking as the project moves forward.

### 6. Town Grants Dark Fiber Licenses to Two Telecom Companies
**Items C7 & C8 | Consent Agenda**

Marana is authorizing dark fiber services license agreements with **MMR Fiber Solutions, LLC** and **Bicentel, LLC**, allowing the companies to use town-owned fiber optic conduit infrastructure. As Marana courts data centers and large commercial tenants, fiber capacity is foundational — and who gets licensed access to municipal infrastructure, and on what terms, is a question worth scrutinizing as the town's digital infrastructure becomes an economic development asset.
