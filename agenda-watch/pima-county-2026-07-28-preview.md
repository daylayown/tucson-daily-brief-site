### 6. 💨 Air Quality Rules Updated to Match Federal Standards — Details Matter
**Item 20 | Public Hearing | Code Text Amendment — Ordinance No. 2026-9**

The Board will hold a public hearing on a sweeping rewrite of Title 17 of the Pima County Code, updating local air quality regulations to incorporate new federal EPA standards covering hazardous air pollutants, acid rain programs, new source performance standards, and National Ambient Air Quality Standards. Pima County has its own air quality department with jurisdiction in the unincorporated county, and how tightly it aligns with (or exceeds) federal rules affects industries, construction, and public health across the region. This is also a moment to ask whether the county is keeping pace with updated particulate matter standards tied to wildfire smoke — an increasingly acute issue in Southern Arizona.

### 7. 🍼 Long Waitlists for County Preschool Program Trigger Policy Discussion

The Board will confront the growing waitlist problem for the Pima Early Education Program (PEEPs), the county's subsidized preschool program, and consider options to expand enrollment. > **Watch for:** Whether any expansion option requires new General Fund spending, given the county's ongoing structural deficit concerns.

### 8. 🚨 Sheriff Lands $780K Federal Drug Task Force Grant — But Immigration Politics Loom
**Item 31 | Grant Acceptance — Sheriff's Office**

The Pima County Sheriff's Department will accept $780,819 from the White House Office of National Drug Control Policy's High Intensity Drug Trafficking Areas (HIDTA) program. HIDTA funding supports multi-agency drug enforcement task forces and often involves coordination with federal law enforcement — a politically charged issue in a border county where the Sheriff's relationship with federal immigration enforcement has been a persistent flashpoint. Residents and advocates will want to know what conditions, if any, are attached to this grant regarding data sharing or joint operations with ICE or DEA.

> **Ongoing story connection:** Any federal grant to PCSD that involves joint task forces should be examined against the backdrop of Pima County's posture on immigration enforcement cooperation.
