Daily Brief

August 4, 2026

Tuesday

🏛️ Government

Tucson says its water supply can absorb pending Colorado River cuts. The U.S. Bureau of Reclamation released its Final Environmental Impact Statement for post-2026 operations of Lake Powell and Lake Mead on July 31, and the city said in a release that Tucson Water is reviewing the document and evaluating implications for the city's Colorado River supplies. City officials told KGUN 9 that Tucson is prepared to adjust to reduced deliveries; KOLD 13 reports Arizona cities and towns are weighing several options as the cuts approach.

City of Tucson, KGUN 9, KOLD News 13

Pima County steps in to fund jail GED program after sheriff cancels contract. The county is paying for a longstanding high school equivalency program at the county jail after Sheriff Chris Nanos ended the contract with Pima Community College, citing costs and unproven results, according to Tucson Spotlight. The report is published in Spanish.

Tucson Spotlight

Voter-approved border enforcement law faces court test as AG hedges. Attorney General Kris Mayes would not commit to defending the legality of the voter-approved measure that allows state and local police to arrest border crossers, the Arizona Daily Star reports. Separately, a federal judge denied a temporary restraining order that would have blocked part of the law, according to the Nogales International.

Arizona Daily Star, Nogales International

City's new homelessness survey asks who is most likely to die outside. Tucson outreach workers have replaced a national questionnaire used for a decade to prioritize housing resources with a locally developed tool built around mortality risk, Arizona Luminaria and AZPM report. The change is intended to steer limited housing help toward people most likely to die while living unsheltered.

Arizona Luminaria, AZPM

🚨 Public Safety

Inmate dies at Pima County jail. The Pima County Sheriff's Department said 39-year-old Jesus M. Tapia was found unresponsive in his cell Monday morning at the Pima County Adult Detention Complex, according to KOLD 13 and KVOA. KOLD described it as another death at the complex; the department said it is investigating.

KOLD News 13, KVOA News 4

Sheriff's department releases ransom notes in Nancy Guthrie case. Deputies on Friday made public two ransom notes received in the case, saying they hope someone recognizes the writer's "unique linguistic style," the Nogales International reports. Volunteers meanwhile continued searching desert terrain in Mexico for Guthrie, the Arizona Daily Star reports.

Nogales International, Arizona Daily Star

Fifth straight extreme heat day strains grid as cooling center gaps persist. Tucson Electric Power said it is managing high demand and the potential for storm-related outages, monitoring the grid around the clock and relying on 10 reciprocating engine generators to meet peaks, according to KOLD 13 and KGUN 9. AZPM reports Pima County cooling centers have served thousands of residents this summer but that significant service gaps remain.

KOLD News 13, KGUN 9, AZPM

Chemical spill in Sonora could reach the San Pedro basin. A hazardous chemical spill in the Upper San Pedro watershed in Sonora could affect the north-flowing river as it enters Arizona, a hydrologist told the Arizona Daily Star. The potential downstream impact in southeastern Arizona has not been quantified.

Arizona Daily Star

🎓 Education

U of A president seeks campus input on federal questionnaire. President Suresh Garimella told the campus community Monday that the university, along with others nationwide, received a communication from U.S. Education Secretary Linda McMahon containing a questionnaire on a broad range of higher education issues. The Arizona Daily Star reports the request follows the White House's higher education compact sent to universities 10 months ago, which listed demands in exchange for preferential research funding.

University of Arizona, Arizona Daily Star

Catalina Foothills students return under phone ban. Catalina Foothills Unified School District students went back to class Monday under a strict "off and away" cellphone policy, KOLD 13 reports. District officials told the station the restriction is showing results.

KOLD News 13

🏗️ Development & Business

Grant Road underpass reopens after railroad project work. Grant Road between the I-10 westbound frontage road and Flowing Wells Road reopened to traffic at 4 a.m. Saturday following major improvements tied to the Grant Road Underpass at the Union Pacific Railroad Project, the city said in a release. Crews with contractor KE&G will remain in the area through the end of August finishing work.

City of Tucson

Nogales port expansion would demolish historic Elm Street. A federal proposal to expand the DeConcini border crossing would raze a historic Nogales neighborhood along Elm Street, Tucson Spotlight reports, drawing objections from residents and city leaders. Federal officials cite safety concerns at the aging port.

Tucson Spotlight

📢 What Your Officials Are Saying

Rep. Adelita Grijalva (D-CD7). Grijalva said in a release that she is condemning the Trump administration for cutting down 100-year-old cottonwood trees to build a second border wall.

Rep. Adelita Grijalva

Rep. Juan Ciscomani (R-CD6). Ciscomani announced a back-to-school release highlighting what his office describes as major federal investments in Southern Arizona schools and workforce training.

Rep. Juan Ciscomani

Sen. Mark Kelly (D-Ariz.). Kelly said in a release that he and Sen. John Curtis are proposing a state-sponsored visa pilot program.

Sen. Mark Kelly

Andy Biggs (R), candidate for governor. The Biggs campaign posted a media roundup characterizing state Sen. Sine Kerr as a "home run" pick for lieutenant governor. Gov. Katie Hobbs' campaign did not post anything in the past 48 hours.

Biggs for Arizona

In the competitive CD6 race, neither JoAnna Mendoza's nor Juan Ciscomani's campaign posted anything in the past 48 hours.
