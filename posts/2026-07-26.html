Daily Brief

July 26, 2026

Sunday

🚨 Public Safety

Son charged with first-degree murder in Vail woman's death. A man faces a first-degree murder charge after his 65-year-old mother was found dead inside their shared Vail-area home, according to the Pima County Sheriff's Department. PCSD Homicide Unit detectives reportedly found concerning evidence at the scene.

KGUN 9, KOLD News 13

Man dies after ejection in southside rollover crash. A 27-year-old man died after being ejected from his vehicle during a single-car rollover on East Benson Highway near South Hartford Stravenue on Friday night, according to KVOA. Speed and distracted driving are listed as contributing factors.

KVOA News 4, KOLD News 13

Jack Upchurch convicted on all counts in Pima County murder case. A jury convicted Jack Upchurch on all charges in the murder of Paul Clifford, according to KOLD News 13. Sentencing is scheduled for September 28.

KOLD News 13

Downtown Tucson businesses reopen to weekend crowds following mass shooting. Bars and restaurants on Congress Street welcomed their first peak nightlife rush since a mass shooting last weekend left nine people injured, according to KOLD. Separately, the Downtown Tucson Partnership is working on a plan to close roads during major events to improve public safety.

KOLD News 13, KVOA News 4

🏛️ Government

Federal judge declines to block Arizona's voter-approved border policing law. A federal judge refused late Friday to block state and local police from enforcing a voter-approved Arizona law that allows officers to arrest individuals who enter the country at locations other than a legal port of entry.

Arizona Daily Star

Amphitheater Public Schools to seek voter approval to sell surplus properties. Amphitheater Public Schools is placing a measure on the November 3 ballot asking voters to authorize the sale, lease, or exchange of land and buildings the district no longer needs, according to Inside Tucson Business.

Inside Tucson Business

Pima County helping residents navigate new federal SNAP work requirements. Pima County is offering assistance to residents affected by newly expanded federal work requirements for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, according to Inside Tucson Business.

Inside Tucson Business

🏗️ Development & Business

UA Tech Park startup converts captured CO₂ into aviation fuel. Dimensional Energy, a Tucson-based company at the UA Tech Park, has developed technology that transforms captured carbon dioxide into renewable oil for use in aviation fuel, diesel, and other products, according to the Arizona Daily Star.

Arizona Daily Star

Empire Pizza and Pub signals it will not expand into downtown Hydra building. Following last week's downtown mass shooting, Empire Pizza and Pub indicated via social media that its planned expansion into the Hydra building is likely off, according to the Arizona Daily Star.

Arizona Daily Star

TMC Health Foundation receives $500,000 grant to expand telehealth access. The James M. Cox Foundation has awarded a $500,000 grant to the TMC Health Foundation to expand virtual health care services at TMC Rincon, with a focus on underserved populations across Southern Arizona, according to Inside Tucson Business.

Inside Tucson Business

🎉 Community & Events

Pima County hosts teen mental health and addiction conference. Pima County held a new conference called "Let's Talk About It," connecting young people with local resources and support services related to mental health and drug addiction prevention, according to KGUN 9.

KGUN 9

'Respiro' wellness event returns to downtown Tucson Sunday evening. The eighth edition of Respiro, a community wellness gathering focused on breathwork and relaxation, is scheduled for Sunday evening at the Presidio San Agustín del Tucson Museum in downtown, according to KGUN 9.

KGUN 9

⛈️ Weather — Tucson

Tucson remains in an extended period of dangerous heat. Today (Sunday) will be mostly sunny with a high near 105°F; heat index values could reach 108°F. West winds 2–9 mph. A slight chance of thunderstorms returns after 5 p.m. Overnight lows will hover near 81–82°F, well above average, with slight storm chances continuing into early Monday.

Monday brings another sunny and hot day, near 104°F (heat index up to 107°F), with afternoon and overnight storm chances increasing — precipitation probability reaching 40% Monday night. Tuesday and Wednesday remain mostly sunny with highs near 104–105°F and only isolated storm chances.

The prolonged above-average heat is expected to persist through at least midweek, with monsoon storm activity remaining scattered and unreliable. Residents should limit outdoor activity during peak afternoon hours and stay hydrated.
