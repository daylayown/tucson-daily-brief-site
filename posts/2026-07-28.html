⛈️ Weather — Tucson

⚠️ Extreme Heat Warning in effect 10 AM Thursday through 8 PM Sunday. NWS Tucson warns of dangerously hot conditions with highs of 107–112°F across the Tucson metro area, Western Pima County, Tohono O'odham Nation, and surrounding regions. Heat-related illness risk is rated Major to Extreme.

Today: Mostly sunny, high near 103°F. Heat index up to 106°F. Slight chance of early morning thunderstorms. North-northwest wind 2–9 mph. Tonight: Partly cloudy, low around 81°F. Heat index values as high as 103°F.

Tomorrow (Wednesday): Sunny, high near 106°F, heat index up to 107°F. Thursday climbs to near 109°F, with Friday forecast at 110°F. The extended pattern brings little to no monsoon relief — temperatures will remain well above seasonal averages through the weekend with dangerous overnight lows in the low-to-mid 80s providing minimal recovery time.

🏛️ Government

Pima County supervisors set to appoint new county treasurer. The Pima County Board of Supervisors is scheduled to meet and appoint a new county treasurer, according to KVOA. No additional details on timing or candidates were immediately available.

KVOA News 4

Rep. Grijalva tours flood-damaged Nogales Wash, presses for federal funding. U.S. Rep. Adelita Grijalva joined Nogales city and county officials Monday to inspect monsoon-damaged sections of the Nogales Wash, including eroded banks, exposed utilities, and the site of a proposed retention basin. Leaders are pushing for federal funding to close an estimated $30 million repair gap in the aging flood control system.

Nogales International, KGUN 9, KOLD News 13

'Sham' Green Party candidates tied to Arizona GOP machine, Arizona Mirror reports. Two candidates running under the Green Party banner — Risa Lombardo and Duwayne Collier — are operating in coordination with Arizona's Republican campaign apparatus and directing large portions of Clean Elections public funding to a newly formed Nevada company, according to the Arizona Mirror. The Arizona Green Party has publicly called them fraudulent candidates.

Arizona Mirror

Sen. Gallego donates Palantir campaign contributions to Arizona immigrant relief fund. U.S. Sen. Ruben Gallego directed $17,500 in campaign contributions received from Palantir executives to the Arizona Undocumented Workers Relief Fund, the Arizona Mirror reports. A Gallego spokesperson confirmed the donation, citing the surveillance company's role as a technology backbone for ICE operations.

Arizona Mirror

🚨 Public Safety

Vail man charged with first-degree murder in mother's death; court documents cite delusion. Andre Tyrone Tillmon, 38, has been charged with first-degree murder in the death of his mother, 65-year-old Cindy Kreider. Court documents indicate Tillmon told Pima County Sheriff's deputies he did not believe his mother was real. Deputies described delusion as a possible factor in the killing.

KGUN 9, KVOA News 4, KOLD News 13

Tucson man sentenced to life in prison for eastside gas station shooting. A Tucson man convicted of fatally shooting a 46-year-old man at an eastside gas station approximately two years ago has been sentenced to life in prison, according to KVOA.

KVOA News 4

Westside residents say illegal gunfire persists despite PCSD's Operation Desert Silence. Neighbors in Eagle Point Estates report continued illegal gunfire in the area even after the Pima County Sheriff's Department launched its targeted enforcement campaign. KGUN 9 spoke with residents who say the problem has not been resolved.

KGUN 9

Pregnant ICE detainee released following Tucson hospitalization. A pregnant Colombian woman was released from ICE custody after spending three weeks in detention, during which she alleged a Border Patrol agent violently assaulted her during her arrest near Douglas, according to the Arizona Daily Star.

Arizona Daily Star

Red Cross declares emergency blood shortage, urges Tucsonans to donate. The American Red Cross Southern Arizona chapter says blood supplies have reached critical levels, with chapter representative Courtney Slanaker attributing the shortage to summer travel, school closures, and natural disasters reducing donor turnout. The organization is calling for year-round donations to stabilize supply.

Arizona Daily Star

🏗️ Development & Business

Grant Road reconstruction project on schedule to open in August. Crews from KE&G Construction, under contract with the City of Tucson, completed milling and paving on Grant Road between Fairview Avenue and Flowing Wells last week. Temporary striping is set to be applied, with the corridor expected to reopen to traffic next month, according to KVOA.

KVOA News 4

Downtown Tucson venues adopt shared ID scanners to flag disruptive patrons. Sixteen downtown Tucson businesses have begun using new ID-scanning devices that share real-time flagging data across participating venues, according to KOLD. The system is intended to help establishments identify and turn away individuals with prior records of disruptive behavior.

KOLD News 13

Code Ninjas opens new location in Oro Valley. A new Code Ninjas coding education franchise has opened in Oro Valley, funded in part by local parents who pooled their own money to bring the location to the community, according to KOLD.

KOLD News 13

University of Arizona posts record year for invention disclosures. The University of Arizona reported a record-breaking number of invention disclosures in the most recent fiscal year, reflecting increased activity in translating university research into commercial applications, according to KVOA. No specific figures were included in available details.

KVOA News 4

🎉 Community & Events

Sahuarita Unified and Pima County jointly address 83% spike in youth suicides. Sahuarita Unified School District and Pima County are working together to respond to an 83% increase in youth suicides recorded between November 2024 and November 2025, according to the Green Valley News. Officials have not yet detailed specific intervention programs in available reporting.

Green Valley News

Border wall construction approaches historic cottonwood grove in Lochiel. Contractors have begun tearing out sections of the existing border fence in Lochiel, about 25 miles east of Nogales, as new wall construction moves toward a stand of historic cottonwood trees that environmentalists describe as a critical ecosystem. The work was observed Tuesday morning by Arizona Public Media, which published the report via AZ Luminaria.
