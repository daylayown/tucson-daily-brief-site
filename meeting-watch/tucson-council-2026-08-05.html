Tucson Mayor & Council Meeting Preview: August 5, 2026

This Wednesday's meeting is relatively light on major spending decisions but features two significant land use and development items that will shape Tucson's economic and neighborhood future. The most consequential action is a public hearing on regulating large-scale data centers — a policy question with major implications for Tucson's water supply, power grid, and economic development trajectory.

Top Items to Watch

🖥️ Tucson Sets Rules for Data Centers — Water and Power Demands at Stake

Item 8 | Ordinance No. 12268 | Public Hearing | C8-25-04

The city will hold a public hearing on new Unified Development Code regulations governing where and how large-scale data centers can be built in Tucson. Data centers are water- and energy-intensive facilities increasingly targeting Sun Belt cities like Tucson for expansion, and this ordinance will define the zoning conditions under which they can operate. This is the most newsworthy item on the agenda. In a city already grappling with Colorado River allocation cuts and summer heat stress on the power grid, how Tucson regulates this industry could have generational consequences for water sustainability and utility rates. Residents can speak at the public hearing — this is a rare opportunity for public input on a policy that will shape Tucson's growth for decades.

Note: Full ordinance text amends UDC Sections 4.9.11 and 11.3.11. Reporters should request the staff report to determine whether the regulations include water-use thresholds, cooling technology requirements, or proximity restrictions near residential areas.

🏗️ Residential Land Near Limberlost Getting Rezoned for Commercial Use

Item 9 | Ordinance No. 12265 | Ward 3

This is a rezoning request to convert a parcel at 4137 N. 1st Ave. — at the southwest corner of Limberlost Drive and 1st Avenue — from R-2 (medium-density residential) to C-1 (neighborhood commercial). The change modifies a previously approved development plan and conditions tied to Ordinance No. 12086. Rezonings from residential to commercial directly affect neighboring property owners through traffic, lighting, noise, and neighborhood character, and this stretch of 1st Avenue is an active corridor connecting midtown to the university area. Ward 3 residents near Limberlost should be aware this could pass without significant public debate if the Planning Commission has already signed off.

🌉 State Funding Deal for 22nd Street Bridge Replacement — But No Details Yet

Item 7b | Consent Agenda | IGA with State of Arizona | Ward 5

The city is set to approve an intergovernmental agreement with the State of Arizona for supplemental construction funding for the 22nd Street bridge replacement between Kino Parkway and Tucson Boulevard. Notably, the agenda states the full material "will be distributed as it becomes available," meaning council members and the public may not have seen the full terms before the vote. Bridge infrastructure in this corridor serves a heavily traveled south-side route connecting Kino-area destinations and residential neighborhoods. The missing documentation is itself a story — residents and council members deserve to know the funding amounts, state conditions, and construction timeline before this lands on a consent vote.

On consent agenda — could pass without discussion.

💼 FY2027 Economic & Workforce Development Funding Allocations Approved

Item 7c | Resolution No. 24158 | Consent Agenda | City Wide

The Economic and Workforce Development Selection Committee's recommended funding allocations for Fiscal Year 2027 are up for council approval, along with an extension of the committee's term through September 1, 2028. These allocations direct city dollars to workforce training programs, small business support, and economic development initiatives — spending that directly affects job creation and working residents. Given Tucson's ongoing challenges with wage growth and economic opportunity, the specific organizations and programs receiving FY27 funds are worth scrutinizing in the full staff report.

On consent agenda — could pass without discussion.

🚒 City's Fire Coverage Contract at Raytheon Plant Gets a Temporary Patch

Item 7g | Resolution No. 24157 | Consent Agenda | City Wide

The council will approve a three-month extension — through October 1, 2026 — of the fire, medical, and special hazard services agreement between the City of Tucson Fire Department and Raytheon's U.S. Air Force Plant 44 manufacturing facility. The original contract expired June 30, 2026, meaning the city has been operating without a fully executed agreement for over a month. Raytheon's Plant 44 is one of Tucson's largest employers and a critical defense manufacturing site, making fire and hazmat response there a significant public safety concern. The short extension window suggests a longer-term contract renegotiation is underway — reporters should ask why this wasn't resolved before the June 30 expiration.

On consent agenda — could pass without discussion.

🎓 City and Pima Community College Formalize Training Partnership

Item 7h | Resolution No. 24159 | Consent Agenda | City Wide

Tucson and Pima County Community College District (PCC) will renew an intergovernmental agreement providing educational and training services, facilities, and opportunities for both the Tucson Fire Department and Tucson Police Department. With both departments facing ongoing staffing challenges — a persistent story in Tucson public safety coverage — formalized training pipelines with PCC are a meaningful piece of the workforce development picture. This IGA is worth a follow-up question: what specific programs are covered, and does this agreement support any new recruit academy capacity?

On consent agenda — could pass without discussion.

🍹 One Liquor License Application Flagged for Non-Compliance

Item 5b(4) | Tortillas Chiwas | Ward 1

Among seven liquor license applications on the agenda, one stands out: Tortillas Chiwas at 914 N. Grande Ave. has been flagged by the Planning and Development Services Department as not in compliance with city requirements, specifically because the location requires prior approval of a Development Package and a Liquor Mitigation Plan — neither of which has been completed. While routine liquor licenses rarely merit coverage, this application could either be denied or trigger a discussion about the city's process for new food and beverage establishments in Ward 1. The other six applications (including two 7-Eleven locations and a bookstore) appear to have cleared staff review.

The Tucson Daily Brief will be at City Hall Wednesday at 5:30 p.m. Reporters seeking the full staff reports for Items 7b, 8, and 9 should contact the City Clerk's office ahead of the meeting.
